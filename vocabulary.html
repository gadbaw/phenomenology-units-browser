Fault Lines in the Relational Field

Naming the Fault Line

How the psychoanalytic traditions describe relational destabilization, the turn, and repair.

A companion reference of the phrases different theorists use for fault-line–like moments, arranged by where each lives in the process. Of 88 terms, 45 carry a definition or characterizing sentence in the theorist's own words, quoted verbatim with page; where wording could not be verified against a source on hand, the quote is omitted rather than guessed.

Precondition

20

Latent vulnerability — the ground in which a fault line can form.

Bad-object tie / moral defense

Fairbairn · British Independent

Loyalty to the bad object; the self made bad to keep the object good

On the moral defence: “If the delinquent child is reluctant to admit that his parents are bad objects, he by no means displays equal reluctance to admit that he himself is bad” (Fairbairn, 1952, p. 65).

Fear of breakdown

Winnicott · British Independent

Dread of a breakdown that, unknowably, has already happened in early life

“…fear of breakdown is the fear of a breakdown that has already been experienced” (Winnicott, 1989, p. 90).

Identity diffusion / primitive defenses / BPO

Kernberg · Borderline/TFP

Unintegrated self-other representations and splitting-based defenses

Identity diffusion is “a poorly integrated concept of the self and of significant others” (Kernberg, 1984, p. 12).

Normative unconscious processes

Layton · Relational

How social norms/hierarchies are unconsciously enacted in the clinical pair

“The ones that seek to maintain the splits are those that I call normative unconscious processes” (Layton, 2006, p. 242).

The basic fault

Balint · British Independent

A structural deficit/area of two-person malformation from early misattunement

“I propose to call it the level of the basic fault, and I wish to stress that it is described as a fault, not as a situation, position, conflict, or complex” (Balint, 1968, p. 16).

Unformulated experience

D. B. Stern · Relational

Experience that has not yet been articulated into words/meaning

“…potential experience, what experience might become” (D. B. Stern, 2017, p. 518).

Beta elements / alpha function

Bion · Bionian/Field

Raw unprocessed sense-impressions vs. the capacity that metabolizes them into thinkable experience

Cumulative trauma

Khan · British Independent

Trauma built from repeated small failures of the protective shield

Fault line in the relational field

Gadbaw (this study) · Meta/Method

Heuristic: a latent zone of relational vulnerability that can destabilize under pressure

Identification with the aggressor

Ferenczi · Classical/Freudian

Traumatized submission in which the victim takes in the aggressor

Internal working models

Bowlby · Attachment/Mentalization

Templates of self-with-other that shape expectation under stress

Second skin

Bick · Kleinian

An adhesive, muscular pseudo-holding standing in for absent containment

Security operations / anxiety

Sullivan · Interpersonal

Maneuvers to ward off anxiety that narrow attention and contact

Selfobject

Kohut · Self psychology

The other experienced as part of/in service to the self's cohesion

The dead mother / work of the negative

Green · Lacanian/French

Decathexis by a depressed object leaving a blank/absence at the core

The enigmatic signifier / message

Laplanche · Lacanian/French

The untranslatable, excessive message of the other implanted in the subject

The primitive edge of experience

Ogden · Bionian/Field

The most archaic, presymbolic strata of experience

The uninvited guest / shared phantasy

Fisher · Couple

Unconscious shared phantasy organizing (and constraining) the marriage

The unthought known

Bollas · British Independent

What is known in the body/relation but not yet available to thought

Theatres of the mind / body; alexithymia

McDougall · Lacanian/French

Unsymbolized conflict staged psychically or somatically

Destabilization

27

The rupture — the moment the relational field gives way.

Affective flooding / the tsunami / self-state collapse

Bromberg · Relational

Overwhelm that swamps reflective capacity and collapses self-state mobility

“The mind/brain organizes its self-states as an anticipatory protective system that tries, proactively, to shut down experiential access to self-states that are disjunctive with the dissociatively limited range of the state that is experienced as ‘me’ at a given moment” (Bromberg, 2011, p. 15).

Alliance rupture (withdrawal / confrontation)

Safran & Muran · Change-process/Rupture-repair

A strain or breakdown in the alliance; patient withdraws or moves against the therapist

“Confrontation ruptures are likely to arouse intense and disturbing feelings of anger, impotence, self-indictment, and even despair in therapists” (Safran & Muran, 2000, p. 154).

Collapse / failure of mentalization

Fonagy, Gergely, Jurist & Target · Attachment/Mentalization

Loss of the capacity to hold mind-in-mind; reversion to psychic equivalence / pretend mode

Reflective functioning is “the process by which we realize that having a mind mediates our experience of the world” (Fonagy et al., 2002, p. 3).

Countertransference enactment

Jacobs; McLaughlin · Relational

The analyst's unconscious action-response that reveals the dyad's unspoken pressures

“…those regressive (defensive) interactions between the pair experienced by either as a consequence of the behavior of the other” (McLaughlin, 1991, p. 595).

Defences of the self / deintegration

Fordham · Jungian

Self-protective withdrawal; the self's de- and re-integration cycle

Of the primary self: “it integrates and deintegrates… [explaining] how consciousness is produced and how an ego is formed in early infancy” (Fordham, 1996, p. 36).

Disintegration anxiety

Kohut · Self psychology

The dread of self-dissolution — the deepest anxiety

“…disintegration anxiety is not only man’s deepest but also, potentially, his most severe anxiety” (Kohut, 1984, p. 213).

Dissociation / not-me / enactment

D. B. Stern · Relational

Unbidden enactment of dissociated, unformulated 'not-me' states in the dyad

“Dissociation is not a matter of ejecting from consciousness an experience that is already created and then distorting it or hiding it away somewhere in the mind” (D. B. Stern, 2017, p. 503).

Doer and done-to / breakdown of mutual recognition

Benjamin · Relational

Collapse from mutual recognition into complementary, coercive twoness

“This relational formation, based on splitting, takes shape as the complementarity of doer and done to, but there are many other permutations: accuser and accused, helpless and coercive, even victim and perpetrator” (Benjamin, 2018, p. 4).

Driving the other crazy / therapeutic symbiosis

Searles · Interpersonal

Unconscious efforts to disorganize the other; and the symbiotic phase of cure

“This stage must occur before this mode of transference relatedness can become resolved into that which I term ‘therapeutic symbiosis,’ from which a healthier individuation can emerge” (Searles, 1979, p. 378).

Impingement

Winnicott · British Independent

Environmental intrusion that disrupts the infant's/patient's going-on-being

“The provision in the environment for conditions that meet the infant’s and small child’s needs, so that development is not interrupted by reactions to impingement (cold, heat, bad holding, faulty handling, starvation, etc.)” (Winnicott, 1989, p. 59).

Nameless dread

Bion · Bionian/Field

Unmetabolized terror left when maternal/analytic containment fails

When reverie fails, the infant reintrojects “not a fear of dying made tolerable, but a nameless dread” (Bion, 1967, p. 116).

Projective identification

Klein (dev. Bion, Ogden, Joseph) · Kleinian

Splitting off and depositing a state into the other, who then feels/enacts it

“The object becomes to some extent a representative of the ego, and these processes are in my view the basis for identification by projection or ‘projective identification’” (Klein, 1946; held e-book ed., page n/a).

Psychic equivalence / pretend mode

Fonagy et al. · Attachment/Mentalization

Inner = outer with no 'as-if'; or decoupled, meaningless mentalizing

“The abandonment of mentalizing leaves them with an internal reality that is dominated by psychic equivalence” (Fonagy et al., 2002, p. 13).

Racial enactment

Leary · Relational

Enactment organized by racial meanings and the social surround

“I use the term racial enactments to designate those interactive sequences that embody the actualization in the clinical situation of cultural attitudes toward race and racial difference” (Leary, 2000, p. 3).

Role-responsiveness

Sandler · Object relations

The analyst is unconsciously drawn into a role the patient's inner world casts

“My contention is that in the analyst’s overt reactions to the patient as well as in his thoughts and feelings what can be called his ‘role-responsiveness’ shows itself, not only in his feelings but also in his attitudes and behaviour, as a crucial element in his ‘useful’ countertransference” (Sandler, 1976, p. 45).

Symbolic equation

Segal · Kleinian

Collapse of symbolization — the symbol is felt to BE the thing

“Where such symbolic equations are formed in relation to bad objects, an attempt is made to deal with them as with the original object, that is by total annihilation and scotomization” (Segal, 1981, p. 54).

Unbinding

Laplanche · Lacanian/French

A drive that undoes the links between libido and its unconscious representations

“…pressing repetitively towards the unbinding that separates the libido from its unconscious representations” (Laplanche, 1999, p. 4).

Affect storms

Kernberg (TFP) · Borderline/TFP

Intense, flooding affect states that dominate the borderline transference

Attacks on linking

Bion · Bionian/Field

Active assault on the links (between thoughts, between people) that make meaning

Confusion of tongues

Ferenczi · Classical/Freudian

Misattunement between the language of tenderness and of passion (child/adult; patient/analyst)

Empathic failure (rupture and repair)

Kohut · Self psychology

Inevitable selfobject failures whose working-through builds structure

Fragmentation

Kohut · Self psychology

Breakup of self-cohesion under empathic failure

Minus-K (-K)

Bion · Bionian/Field

The active evasion of knowing/truth; an anti-emotional, anti-link stance

Paranoid-schizoid <-> depressive (Ps<->D)

Klein (Bion's Ps<->D) · Kleinian

Oscillation between fragmentation/persecution and integration/concern

Parataxic distortion

Sullivan · Interpersonal

Distortion of the present interpersonal field by earlier patterns

Repetition compulsion / acting out

Freud · Classical/Freudian

Reliving the past in action rather than remembering it

Unbearable / unvalidated affect

Stolorow · Self psychology

Trauma as affect that could not be held within a responsive bond

The turn

9

The moment of change — toward recognition, or toward foreclosure.

Mutative interpretation

Strachey · Classical/Freudian

The interpretation that produces real structural (not just intellectual) change

“…the first criterion of a mutative interpretation would be that it must be a transference interpretation” (Strachey, 1934, p. 155).

The moral / rhythmic third

Benjamin · Relational

The shared 'thirdness' whose restoration re-establishes recognition

“I consider this early exchange to be a form of thirdness, and suggest that we use the term rhythmic Third for the principle of affective attunement and accommodation to shared patterning that informs such exchanges” (Benjamin, 2018, p. 30).

The present moment / vitality affects

D. N. Stern · Change-process/Rupture-repair

The lived now as the unit of change; vitality affects as its texture

“Temporal contours and vitality affects are at the heart of microtemporal dynamics, and the sense of dramatic tension that is crucial to lived stories” (Stern, 2004, p. 62).

Catastrophic change

Bion · Bionian/Field

Growth experienced as violent upheaval that disrupts the existing order

Mutative turn vs. impasse / foreclosure

Gadbaw (this study) · Meta/Method

The fork: a turn that opens toward change vs. one that forecloses

Now moments / moments of meeting

Boston Change Process Study Group · Change-process/Rupture-repair

A charged 'now moment' that, if authentically met, becomes a moment of meeting that shifts implicit relational knowing

The therapeutic action / ghosts into ancestors

Loewald · Object relations

Change as a new object-relationship that transforms old internalizations

The transcendent function

Jung · Jungian

The bridging of conscious/unconscious opposites that generates a new third position

The turn

Gadbaw (this study) · Meta/Method

The moment a destabilized relationship moves toward recognition/symbolization or hardens into impasse

Impasse

8

Foreclosure — when the turn hardens rather than opens.

Addiction to near-death

Joseph · Kleinian

A masochistic pull toward deadlock and despair that fascinates the patient

“…a constant pull towards despair and near-death, so that the patient is fascinated and unconsciously excited by the whole process” (Joseph, 1982, p. 456).

Collusion

Willi · Couple

Reciprocal interlocking of partners' defenses into a fixed system

“…collusion, the term we have used to describe the unconscious interplay of partners resulting from a similar type of fixation upon traumatic childhood experiences” (Willi, 1982, p. 243).

Destructive narcissism

Rosenfeld · Kleinian

An internal gang/organization that attacks the dependent, needy self and the link to the analyst

“In 1971 I called this ‘destructive narcissism’, by which I meant that destructive aspects of the self are idealized and submitted to; they capture and trap the positive dependent aspects of the self” (Rosenfeld, 1987, p. 109).

Marital collusion / unconscious complementarity

Dicks · Couple

Partners lock into reciprocal unconscious roles that resist change

“…the need for unconscious complementariness, a kind of division of function by which each partner supplied part of a set of qualities, the sum of which created a complete dyadic unit” (Dicks, 1967/1993, p. 69).

Psychic retreats

Steiner · Kleinian

A withdrawal to a protected internal position when contact with the analyst feels threatening

A retreat “provides the patient with an area of relative peace and protection from strain when meaningful contact with the analyst is experienced as threatening” (Steiner, 1993, p. 1).

Self-care system / archetypal (self-care) defenses

Kalsched · Jungian

A trauma-driven internal system that 'protects' by attacking links to life and the analyst

“…the archetypal ‘self-care system’ typical of early trauma and its attendant dissociation” (Kalsched, 1998, p. 6).

Foreclosure (forclusion) / the Real

Lacan · Lacanian/French

What is foreclosed from the symbolic returns in the real; the unsymbolizable

Negative therapeutic reaction

Freud (also Riviere, Rosenfeld) · Classical/Freudian

Worsening in response to progress; cure resisted out of unconscious guilt or destructiveness

Repair

7

Aftermath — the restoration of contact and symbolization.

Countertransference disclosure / use of affect

Maroda · Relational

The disciplined, expressive use of the analyst's affect to move stuck process

“…countertransference disclosure can be valuable and effective in working with all patients, not just those with personality disorders” (Maroda, 1991, p. 4).

Marriage as a psychological container

Ruszczynski · Couple

The couple relationship itself as a container for projective processes

“In saying that marriage can be used as a container in which people can develop their own wholeness, we are not saying that it is necessarily used by everyone” (Ruszczynski, 1993, p. 121).

Reverie

Ogden (after Bion) · Bionian/Field

The analyst's drifting receptive state through which unformulated experience is gathered

“When the mother is capable of ‘reverie’ (containment of the infant’s projected feeling-state), a modified version of the original feeling is reinternalized by the infant in a form that can be attributed meaning” (Ogden, 1992, p. 190).

Standing in the spaces

Bromberg · Relational

Capacity to move among self-states while feeling one self — the goal of the work

“…the ability to stand in the spaces between realities without losing any of them—the capacity to feel like one self while being many” (Bromberg, 1998, p. 274).

The internal supervisor / trial identification

Casement · British Independent

Reflective monitoring of one's countertransference while in the work

The therapist uses “this island of intellectual contemplation as the mental space within which the internal supervisor can begin to operate” (Casement, 1985, p. 28).

Container / contained

Bion · Bionian/Field

The capacity to receive, hold, and detoxify projected states

Holding

Winnicott · British Independent

The reliable provision that lets unintegration be borne

Meta

17

Field, frame, and method — how the whole process is conceived.

Mutuality and asymmetry

Aron · Relational

The relationship is mutual but not symmetrical; both minds meet

“My own position is that psychoanalysis is inevitably asymmetrical, perhaps most importantly because there are differences in power between patient and analyst” (Aron, 1996, p. 98).

Object relations couple therapy

Scharff & Scharff · Couple

Couple work guided by transference, countertransference, and projective identification

Object relations couple therapy rests on “the use of transference and countertransference as central guidance mechanisms” (Scharff & Scharff, 2014, p. 3).

The analytic third

Ogden · Bionian/Field

A co-created intersubjective entity carrying what neither party can yet hold alone

“…the unarticulated (and often not yet felt) experience of the analysand as they are taking form in the unconscious intersubjectivity of the analytic pair (i.e., in the analytic third)” (Ogden, 2004, p. 184).

The couple state of mind / the creative couple

Morgan · Couple

A shared third capacity to think about the relationship from within it

“The capacity of the couple to hold a couple state of mind… [shows] as the couple starts to think together about what is happening between them” (Morgan, 2019; page not resolved in held copy).

The interactive field / subtle body

Schwartz-Salant · Jungian

A shared somatic-imaginal field in which transformation occurs

Relatedness can regress into “a hopeless muddle of fusion that blurs any subject-object differentiation” (Schwartz-Salant, 2003, p. 13).

The map is not the territory

Korzybski · Meta/Method

Any model (incl. 'fault line') is a representation, not the clinical reality itself

“A map is not the territory it represents, but, if correct, it has a similar structure to the territory” (Korzybski, 1933, as cited in Ringstrom, 2014, p. 65).

The plural psyche / the mundus imaginalis

Samuels · Jungian

The psyche as irreducibly multiple; the mundus imaginalis as an imaginal 'place' where image and the concrete intermingle

“…the mundus imaginalis as a place where the concrete and the imagistic are intermingled; ‘subtle body’ being the best term to describe this” (Samuels, 1989, p. 94).

Transference as a total situation

Joseph · Kleinian

The whole relationship — not just verbal content — as the field of enactment

“It seems to me that the notion of total situations is fundamental to our understanding and our use of the transference today, and it is this I want to explore further” (Joseph, 1985, p. 447).

Bipersonal field / Type A-B-C

Langs · Jungian/Comm.

Two-person field; Type A symbolic, Type B action-discharge, Type C barrier/foreclosure

Irreducible subjectivity of the analyst

Renik · Relational

The analyst's subjectivity is inevitable and shapes the work; anonymity is a myth

Narrative derivatives / the field

Ferro · Bionian/Field

Patient's talk read as derivatives of the here-and-now emotional field; transformation in dreaming

Secured-symbolizing field

Goodheart · Jungian

A field state in which symbolic communication is restored (vs. discharge/confusion)

Structures of subjectivity / organizing principles

Stolorow & Atwood · Self psychology

Pre-reflective unconscious principles organizing experience within an intersubjective field

The analytic field / bastion

Baranger & Baranger · Bionian/Field

Shared unconscious phantasy of the pair; the 'bastion' is a fortified collusive deadlock

The fallacy of understanding

Levenson · Interpersonal

Privileging transformation/experience over decoding 'meaning'

The intimate room / de-animation

Civitarese · Bionian/Field

The analytic field as a shared sensory-emotional space; loss vs. restoration of vitality

The wounded healer

Sedgwick · Jungian

The analyst's own woundedness as the embodied medium of the cure
